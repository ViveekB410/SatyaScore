## 2. Annotation protocol and provenance

- Protocol: the annotator reviewed **every** AI-suggested verdict and wrote a verdict only when they disagreed; a blank field means "reviewed and agreed". 360 of 456 gold labels (79%) are reviewed-and-agreed; the rest are overrides or confirmations ({'ai_agreed': 360, 'human_override': 93, 'human_confirmed': 3}). These are reviewed labels, not unreviewed ones.
- **Limitation — anchoring:** review happened with the AI verdict visible, which biases toward agreement. Single annotator; no adjudication (see `ANNOTATION_PROVENANCE.md`).
- Round-1 evidence: annotators labelling blind matched the gold labels 73% (B) and 36% (A) of the time on the 56 IAA claims.
- Round-2 measures anchoring directly (section 4).

## 3. Data quality (claim extraction)

- Gold set: 38 of 266 English claims (14.3%) are broken extraction artifacts (no subject + finite verb, a lost %, or too short); Hindi (sentence-level) is clean. Well-formed working set = 418 claims.
- Live extractor fixed (IMPROVEMENT_PLAN 1.1): re-extracting all English source answers, broken claims fell from 38/266 (14.3%) to 0/204 (0.0%). The gold claims were annotated as extracted and are not re-extracted (`results/extraction_quality.md`).

## 9. Defensible findings

1. Multi-signal fusion does **not** beat NLI alone for this task/data.
2. Knowledge-graph verification is **inapplicable** to these descriptive claims (0% coverage).
3. Semantic similarity measures **topicality, not truth** (below chance in Hindi).
4. Detecting **naturally-occurring** hallucinations is hard; no configuration clears a trivial majority baseline on this data.
5. Rule-based English claim decomposition produced broken fragments; a sentence-level safety net removes them (section 3).

## 10. Limitations

- The grouped test split has only **EN 8, HI 2** Hallucinated claims — too few to interpret Hallucinated-F1 in either language.
- All responses were generated by a single LLM (llama-3.3-70b-versatile), which limits generalisation to other models.
- Label reliability: single primary annotator, anchored review (section 2); round-1 3-class agreement is low because the annotators used different label schemes; binary kappa is the reliable figure.
- Dataset small (456) vs the PRD's 3,000; Verified-heavy.
- NLI is a pretrained mDeBERTa stand-in, not the PRD's DeBERTa-v3 + IndicXNLI fine-tune.
- KG signal non-functional on this claim type; Marathi out of scope; baseline suite partial.
